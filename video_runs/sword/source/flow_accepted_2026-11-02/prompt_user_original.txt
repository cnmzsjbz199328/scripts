Create a 6-second image-to-video animation for a 2D side-view fighting game.

REFERENCE INPUTS:
Use ref_sword_pad.png as the exact character design reference and starting
pose. Use sword_attack.mp4 only as a reference for the existing overhead
downward sword cut, body mechanics and visual style.

If start-frame and end-frame controls are available, use the same padded
reference image for both.

Do not use a previously generated frame with cropped feet or a cropped
weapon as the starting image.

CHARACTER IDENTITY:
Preserve the red-haired female swordswoman exactly as shown:
same face, long red hair, checkered headband, white martial arts outfit,
bare feet, wooden bokken, body proportions, colors and painterly anime style.

The ONLY intentional anatomical change is one pair of small canine ears
gradually emerging from her hair and later disappearing.
The canine fur matches her existing red hair.
Preserve the headband and original human facial structure.
No tail, muzzle, claws, fur-covered body, muscle growth or costume changes.

The ears are anatomical, not costume accessories.
They suggest an alert, fierce hunting dog rather than a cute pet.
In side profile, the far canine ear may be partly hidden by the near ear.
Do not turn the head toward the camera to display both ears.

FRAMING AND SAFETY MARGINS — CRITICAL:
Adapt the reference to the output aspect ratio by extending the solid green
background. Never crop the reference or enlarge the character to fill
the frame.

The character's head-to-foot height should occupy approximately 55–60%
of the image height.
Keep the soles at approximately 80% of the image height measured from
the top, leaving approximately 20% empty green space below the feet.

All visible parts, including the raised sword tip, canine ears, flowing
hair, hands and both feet, must remain inside the central 80% of the frame
throughout the entire animation.
Leave at least 10% empty green clearance from every image edge.

Fit the entire motion envelope, including the highest raised sword
position and lowest cutting position, inside this safe area.
If more room is needed, make the entire character smaller before animation.
Never change character scale or camera framing during the clip.

The entire wooden sword and both feet must remain fully visible in every
frame. No cropping, no edge contact and no automatic reframing.

CAMERA AND ORIENTATION:
Static locked orthographic side view.
The character faces screen LEFT throughout the entire clip.
Maintain the reference's left-facing side profile.
The face, chest and pelvis must never turn toward the viewer.
No front view, no three-quarter turn, no rotation into screen depth.

All sword movement occurs within the vertical plane parallel to the screen.
Both feet remain on the same fixed horizontal baseline.
No forward travel, dash, jump or sliding across the frame.

ACTION — ONE COMPLETE SEQUENCE:

0.0–0.5 seconds — NORMAL START:
Hold the exact normal battle stance from the starting reference.
No canine ears are visible.
Keep the eyes open and focused to the LEFT.

0.5–2.0 seconds — SUBTLE CHARGING TRANSFORMATION:
Tighten the two-handed grip on the wooden bokken.
Bend the knees slightly and settle the weight while retaining the existing
high overhead sword stance.
Use only small posture adjustments.
Do not deeply crouch, hunch onto all fours or introduce a large wind-up.

One pair of small canine ears gradually grows out of the hair.
Show intermediate stages: small emerging tips, partially formed ears,
then complete small canine ears.
Do not make the ears appear in a single sudden pop.
As they develop, the ears angle slightly backward.
Keep the face, body size, clothing and weapon unchanged.

2.0–3.0 seconds — CHARGED HOLD:
Maintain the completed canine-ear form and overhead ready stance.
Both hands remain high near or above the head.
The sword tip is above and slightly behind the head.
Use only subtle breathing and very small natural hair and ear movement.
Keep both hands firmly attached to the same sword grip.
No additional transformation develops.

3.0–3.2 seconds — COMPRESSED ANTICIPATION:
Briefly tighten the overhead stance.
Sink the knees a little further and load the rear leg.
Keep the hands high and draw the elbows slightly back.
The canine ears press backward slightly.
Keep the eyes open with a fierce, concentrated expression.
Hold this compressed pose for a short, clearly readable beat.

The small movement restriction applies to charging and transformation.
It must NOT weaken or shorten the following sword cut.

3.2–3.65 seconds — EXPLOSIVE OVERHEAD DOWNWARD CUT:
Perform ONE forceful, committed downward sword cut toward screen LEFT.

Suddenly drive through the legs and transfer weight onto the front foot.
The hips initiate the force, followed immediately by the torso, shoulders,
elbows and both hands.
The whole body drives the sword; this is not an arms-only swing.

Use clear acceleration:
start from the held overhead position, accelerate rapidly through the
cutting arc, then decelerate firmly into the finishing pose.
Do not move at a slow constant speed.
Do not gently lower the sword.

The hands descend from above the head, passing in front of the face
and chest, and finish in front of the waist.
The sword tip follows a steep downward arc toward the lower LEFT.
Finish with the sword tip pointing down-left and lower than the hands.

The cut must read as an overhead downward chop.
It must NOT become a horizontal waist-level sweep, sideways slash,
spinning attack or thrust.

Maintain the left-facing side profile.
Do not rotate the chest toward the camera to perform the attack.
Keep the feet within their original footprint, allowing only a small,
natural heel lift during weight transfer.

The canine ears remain fully present throughout the cut.
Animate only the physical character and wooden sword.
Do not draw sword energy or a projectile.

3.65–4.0 seconds — STRONG FINISHING HOLD:
The front knee bends firmly to receive the weight.
The rear leg braces and the torso inclines forward moderately.
Stop both hands decisively in front of the waist.
Keep a slight natural bend in the elbows.
The sword points down-left, with its tip lower than the hands.

Hair and loose sleeves follow the physical motion:
they lag behind the initial acceleration, then continue forward briefly
as the body stops and settle naturally.
No exaggerated stretching or enlarged hair volume.

Hold the completed cutting pose visibly before recovering.
Create a clear impression of weight, control and a completed strike.
Do not immediately bounce the sword back up.

4.0–5.5 seconds — RECOVERY AND REVERSE TRANSFORMATION:
Recover naturally from the finishing pose.
Shift the weight back into the original balanced stance and raise the
wooden sword back to its exact starting position.

During recovery, the canine ears gradually shorten and disappear back
into the hair.
Show the anatomical reversal clearly.
Do not hide the change behind effects, a head turn or a camera movement.
Do not reverse-play the attack.

Restore the original hair silhouette and normal human appearance.
Keep the same face, clothing, weapon and body proportions.

5.5–6.0 seconds — EXACT NORMAL END:
Hold the original normal battle stance with no canine ears.
Match the starting frame's pose, sword angle, hand positions, foot
positions, character scale and screen position as closely as possible.
The final pose must connect seamlessly to the normal idle animation.
No extra attack, flourish or movement after returning to the start pose.

ANIMATION QUALITY:
The force comes from compressed anticipation, sudden acceleration,
coordinated whole-body movement and a firm finishing hold.
Use crisp, readable frames with clean edges and zero motion blur.
Keep the physical wooden sword consistent in length, shape and rigidity.
Both hands must maintain a believable continuous grip.

BACKGROUND AND EFFECTS:

Every pixel outside the character and her wooden sword must be solid green.
Flat even lighting.
No ground, floor, platform, cast shadow, scenery, text or watermark.

Only animate the character, her anatomical canine ears, her existing

Absolutely no sword beam, white crescent, glowing blade, slash trail,
